Please fit the calcium oscillation trace in calcium.csv to the Borghans, Dupont and Goldbeter model. I am interested in the coupled feedback between cytosolic calcium, the internal store and IP3, rather than fitting an empirical periodic curve.

Time is in minutes. The three states are Z (cytosolic calcium), Y (calcium in the sensitive store) and A (IP3). Define the pump, release and IP3 breakdown rates as follows; ^ means a power:

pump = Vm2 Z^2 / (K2^2 + Z^2)
release = Vm3 A^4 Y^2 Z^4 / ((A^4 + Ka^4)(Ky^2 + Y^2)(Kz^4 + Z^4))
breakdown = Vd A^2 Z^n_par / ((Kd^n_par + Z^n_par)(A^2 + Kp^2))

dZ/dt = v0 + beta_par v1 - pump + release + Kf Y - K_par Z
dY/dt = pump - release - Kf Y
dA/dt = Vp beta_par - breakdown - epsilon_par A

At t = 0 use Z = 0.087920524425504, Y = 0.999348084438687 and A = 0.99999999999996. Hold these initial concentrations fixed; they come from an existing reference calibration. The first measurement is slightly later than zero, so integrate from zero and compare at the actual observation times.

The columns in calcium.csv are time and Ca. Ca is the measured signal, whose prediction is offset + scale Z. There are no measured input signals driving the equations.

Fit K2, K_par, Ka, Kd, Kf, Kp, Ky, Kz, Vd, Vm2, Vm3, Vp, beta_par, epsilon_par, n_par, v0 and v1, as well as the signal offset and scale. Use logarithmic parameter scales and bounds 0.001 to 100000 for each of these 19 parameters, matching the broad reference search range. Do not fit the initial conditions or a noise parameter.

For this fitting task, use RMSE in the measured signal, divided by the largest absolute measured Ca value. Keep all observations. A stiff solver is appropriate. With only one measured signal, please distinguish a usable numerical fit from evidence that all the kinetic parameters have been determined.
